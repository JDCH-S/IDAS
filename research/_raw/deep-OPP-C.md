HEADLINE (read this first): the thesis survives on demand and price, but two findings materially weaken it. (1) REGULATORY: art. 3, 5° of the Belgian law of 17 March 2019 reserves, to ITAA-registered accountants, the "analysis by accounting techniques of the situation and functioning of enterprises from the viewpoint of their creditworthiness, profitability and risks" when performed as a self-employed person for third parties; ITAA prosecutes (2022: three wins; Dec 2024: Kortrijk court, 6 months suspended prison + fine; end-2025: ITAA asked all prosecutors to prioritise criminal prosecution). A non-ITAA founder selling buy-side financial analysis of a target is, on the face of the text, inside the reserved perimeter. (2) COMPETITION: a Belgian solo operator (Analyzediz, Flanders) already sells "independent and affordable" small-deal due diligence with a two-week target, and a Belgian platform (Syno M&A) pre-audits SMEs before listing — the "uncrowded" claim from U-064 is weaker than stated. The demand side is confirmed stronger than U-064 assumed (Wallonia alone: 519 accompanied transmissions in 2024; Belgium ~20,000 businesses/yr facing succession; Vlerick 2026: small deals rebounding, succession the primary driver) and Wallonia subsidises 75% of buyer DD fees up to EUR 15k via Chèques-Transmission — but only through Wallonie Entreprendre-labelled providers.

## 1. Exact problem and exact customer

Problem (VERIFIED, multiple summaries): below ~EUR 10M, Belgian acquisitions are facilitated by accountants/brokers with "financial and legal basics" diligence (masynergy.eu, A-20); Belgian and Dutch practitioner content states the scope of DD is often not set in advance, findings are not carried into the SPA, and buyers over-rely on seller statements (kmoinsider.be; icci.be). No legal obligation to perform DD in Belgium (companeo.be). Sellers of small businesses trade at 4.3-5.1x EBITDA (Vlerick), so a EUR 2M deal has ~EUR 400k EBITDA; a 10-20% normalisation error is EUR 40-80k of EBITDA, i.e. EUR 200-400k of price at 5x (ESTIMATE). German FDD practitioners report structural deviations "in the majority" of small transactions; one example cut implied equity value 38% (onlinebilanz.de, VERIFIED as a vendor example). French sources claim a good DD lowers price 10-30% (viaduc.tech, VERIFIED as a claim, not a study).

## 6. Why now; why incumbents have not; why buyers would buy

Why now (VERIFIED signals): succession wave (185k self-employed 55+; a quarter of family firms transferring in 5 years; external sales now exceed intra-family); small-deal rebound and 3.8-6.1 bidders per small target (Vlerick 2026) — more competitive processes shorten DD windows and favour fast fixed-price work; search-fund/ETA culture arriving in Belgium since 2021 with Vlerick, Bird & Bird and public institutions engaging; Wallonia's 75% DD subsidy and rising accompanied volumes (+21% 2023-24); AI tooling makes ledger ingestion and drafting cheap (category forming 2025-26); Belgian B2B e-invoicing via Peppol since 1 Jan 2026 (founder-facts) means structured sales/purchase data will exist for every target from FY2026 onwards (INFERENCE: cleaner ingestion within 2-3 years).

Why incumbents have not: mid-tier economics are hourly and partner-led; a EUR 5k engagement does not cover their risk/insurance and independence procedures (INFERENCE, consistent with U-064); brokers are seller-paid and conflicted; banks analyse for their own credit decision, not for the buyer; accountants of the buyer often also serve the seller in the same town (independence) — ENF as data, INFERENCE from practitioner content. Counter-evidence: NL mid-tier (Bol) already productised a "verkort boekenonderzoek" in 2026; Analyzediz exists in Flanders; so the gap is closing from both sides.

Why buyers would buy: price (subsidised to ~EUR 1-2k net in Wallonia), speed (two weeks vs 4-8), a document the bank and the notary can put in the file, a negotiating lever (10-30% price impact claims), and independence from the seller's broker. Why they might not: they trust their own accountant, they do not know the product exists, the seller resists data access for a "non-professional", and the buyer's lawyer prefers reps & warranties over DD (INFERENCE).

## 8. Belgium analysis

- Advantage: dense succession wave; three regional programmes with money (Wallonia 75% chèque up to EUR 15k; Brussels 60% prime; Flanders PMV guarantee); public matching platforms; Vlerick ETA ecosystem in Brussels; small multiples mean buyers are price-sensitive and value a cheap DD (INFERENCE); notarial deed for share transfers in BV/SRL creates a natural checkpoint (VERIFIED general).
- Disadvantage: the ITAA reserved-activity regime (sect. 7) — Belgium is stricter than NL/UK (INFERENCE); market split in three languages; Flanders is the largest and highest-priced deal market (5.1x; Overnamemarkt, VLAIO, UNIZO, BVOB are Dutch-first) — DUTCH MATTERS for >50% of the market (ESTIMATE); Brussels deals are small (affairesasuivre targets turnover < EUR 500k) and Brussels consultancy subsidies were suspended Aug 2026 (founder-facts).
- Neutral: Wallonia is the most structured (WE labelling, chèques, 519 accompanied deals) and FR-speaking — the natural beachhead for an EN/FR founder, but the labelling requires 3 years of documented experience and the WE partner directory already lists competitors.
- NL vs FR: FR-only founder reaches ~35-40% of deal flow (ESTIMATE). Reports in Dutch would be needed for Flemish banks/buyers; LLM translation helps but client calls are in Dutch.

## 9. EU expansion (NL, FR, LU, DE, UK)

- NL: largest neighbouring MKB transfer market (Brookz barometer; CBS data); adviser costs EUR 20-100k per deal; "verkort boekenonderzoek" already offered by mid-tier; RA/AA titles protected but financial analysis for buyers not a reserved activity (INFERENCE — verify); language Dutch. Best product-market fit for tooling; hardest for the founder linguistically.
- FR: expert-comptable monopoly covers bookkeeping; audit d'acquisition sold at EUR 10-25k forfaits (VERIFIED) — price umbrella for a EUR 5-8k product; whether a non-EC may sell buyer-side financial analysis: INFERENCE that "conseil" is free but verify with the Ordre; CRA network of repreneurs; language fit good.
- LU: tiny deal count; ENF on pricing; cross-border buyers from BE/FR; skip.
- DE: Red-Flag-FDD for EV < EUR 2M recommended by practitioners (VERIFIED); WP/StB reservation covers audit/tax not FDD (INFERENCE); Nachfolge wave huge; language barrier.
- UK: unregulated, GBP 5-15k SME band (VERIFIED), crowded with fixed-fee providers and the search-fund community; only as a tooling market.
What changes: the regulatory constraint is Belgium-specific in its severity; the subsidy lever is Wallonia-specific; data plumbing (NBB XBRL) is Belgium-specific and must be rebuilt per country (KvK, Infogreffe, Bundesanzeiger, Companies House).

## 10. AI architecture and build effort

Pipeline (human judgement retained at every gate):
1. Intake: engagement letter, scope, NDA; automated data-request list by deal type (share vs asset).
2. Public layer: NBB XBRL/JSON for target + peers (free via Consult; automated via webservice EUR 3.3-12.4k/yr) -> 3-5 year standardised P&L/BS, ratios, filing delays, auditor changes; KBO/BCE, Staatsblad publications, UBO checks (Companyweb/Graydon if budget).
3. Private layer: general ledger / trial balance export (Exact Online, Yuki, WinBooks, Octopus, Horus, BOB — market shares ENF), CODA or CSV bank statements (via Ponto PSD2 with seller consent, or files), VAT returns, payroll summaries, aged debtors/creditors, sales by customer.
4. Normalisation: LLM-assisted chart-of-accounts mapping to the Belgian PCMN/MAR, reconciliation of ledger to filed accounts and to bank cash; rule-based checks (cut-off, related-party accounts 416/489, owner remuneration, rent to owner-held property, one-offs).
5. Analysis engine (deterministic, pandas): revenue by customer concentration, monthly seasonality, gross-margin bridge, EBITDA normalisation schedule, net debt / debt-like items, working-capital peg, cash-conversion, proof of cash; anomaly flags (Benford on sales invoices, round-number payments, month-end spikes, VAT-turnover mismatch).
6. Report generation: LLM drafts FR/NL/EN narrative from the computed tables with citations to schedules; human writes the judgement sections (sustainability of earnings, key risks, price/SPA implications).
7. QA gate: reviewer (ITAA/IBR signatory) signs; version control; audit trail of every AI transformation.
Build effort (ESTIMATE): MVP scripts for NBB parsing + ledger normalisation + report template: 80-150 hours; robust multi-software ingestion and bank reconciliation: +150-300 hours; adviser-facing web tool with data room upload and workflow: +400-800 hours (needs a developer). LLM cost per report: negligible (< EUR 20, ESTIMATE). Evidence that vendors state "no AI tool runs a full QoE" and that AI automates bank reconciliation/TB mapping "while preserving advisor judgment": VERIFIED (workwisesolutions, qoeagent).

## 11. AI leverage, commoditisation, AGI, ASI tests

- AI leverage: HIGH. 40-60% of analyst hours (ingestion, mapping, drafting, translation) are automatable today (ESTIMATE); this is what makes a EUR 5k fixed fee viable at 15-20 h/report.
- AI commoditisation: HIGH on the analysis, LOW on the accountability. Honest answer: yes, a buyer can drop a trial balance and bank export into an LLM and get a plausible normalised-EBITDA memo today; current models still hallucinate and, critically, cannot evidence claims to an audit standard ("most AI-generated reports fail audits" — VERIFIED as a vendor/analyst claim, rillet.com; medium.com test). What the buyer cannot get from an LLM: independent responsibility, a signature a bank/notary accepts, access to the seller's data under NDA, the management interview, and the legal right (in Belgium) to have a third party perform 5°. Over 3-5 years the analysis layer's price trends to zero; the durable revenue is the signed, insured opinion and the channel. Classification: the product must be sold as "independent verified opinion + data access + speed", not as "analysis".
- AGI test: if AGI arrives, ingestion/analysis/drafting are free and instantaneous; the residual is legal accountability under the 2019 law and buyer trust; a non-ITAA founder is then competing on nothing. The tooling-for-advisers path collapses too unless it owns proprietary data (Belgian small-deal benchmarks) — weak moat. Classification: FRAGILE.
- ASI test: moot; the reserved-profession framework would be the last thing standing and may itself be reformed.
- Net: AI is necessary for the unit economics but is also the reason the standalone analysis will not hold price; the defensible layer belongs to licensed professionals, which the founder is not.

## 12. Business model and unit economics

Fixed fee (ESTIMATE ladder): EUR 3,500 / 5,500 / 8,500 excl. VAT by EV band (sect. 3). Hours per report (ESTIMATE): intake and data chase 3 h; public-data pull 0.5 h; ledger ingestion and normalisation 3-5 h with tooling (8-10 h manual); analysis 6-8 h; management call 1.5 h; drafting 2-3 h with LLM; QA and signatory review 2 h; delivery call 1 h => 19-24 h with tooling, 28-35 h manual. Effective rate at EUR 5,500 / 22 h = EUR 250/h before signatory share; if an ITAA/IBR partner signs and takes 30-40% (ESTIMATE), founder nets ~EUR 3,300-3,850 = EUR 150-175/h — still above the EUR 80-150/h accountant benchmark.
Capacity at 10-15 h/wk: 2-3 reports/month if perfectly sequenced, realistically 1-2 because DD windows cluster and the two-week SLA collides with the day job => 15-25 reports/yr => EUR 80-140k gross, EUR 50-90k net of signatory share (ESTIMATE). Margin on direct costs > 85% (insurance, data, LLM).
Paths: EUR 1k/month = 2-3 reports/yr (validation phase, achievable). EUR 10k/month = ~22 reports/yr at EUR 5.5k — the ceiling of the part-time model; requires an ITAA partner and one steady referral channel. EUR 100k/month = 18-20 reports/month => 4-6 analysts + signatories (a boutique of 8-10 people) OR an adviser-tooling SaaS at EUR 300-500/month for 200-300 firms across BE+NL+FR (ESTIMATE) — neither is reachable part-time; both require leaving employment and capital.
Four capital scenarios: (a) EUR 0-2k: sole proprietorship under VAT franchise, templates, Python scripts, LLM API, RC pro; sell via an ITAA partner's letterhead. (b) EUR 10-25k: SRL/BV, WE labelling attempt, NBB Improved Data (EUR 3.3k), Companyweb/Graydon, website FR/NL/EN, legal opinion on art. 3 (EUR 2-4k, ESTIMATE). (c) EUR 100k: hire or partner a certified accountant, build the ingestion tool, NBB webservice (EUR 12.4k/yr), first sales push in NL. (d) EUR 500k+: adviser SaaS with a developer team, cross-border data, NL/FR launch — only if the tooling wedge is validated with 10+ paying advisers first.

Manual: engagement letter, NDA, data request, management call, judgement sections, delivery. Automate first: NBB XBRL -> standardised 5-year statements and ratio pack (public data, no seller needed, demoable to any adviser on any target — this is also the lead magnet); trial-balance mapping + normalisation schedule; report skeleton FR/NL/EN. ## 15. Service -> product -> platform trajectory

- Year 1: 5-12 reports under a signatory; scripts for NBB + ledger; validate price and the two-week promise; secure WE labelling attempt (needs 3 years' evidence — the founder's controlling years may count; ENF).
- Year 2: 15-25 reports; a repeatable "buyer pack" and "bank-ready pack"; first adviser using the NBB/ledger tool on their own deals (pilot pricing EUR 200-400/month, ESTIMATE); decide: boutique with a certified partner vs tooling.
- Year 3: either a 3-4 person boutique (EUR 300-500k revenue, ESTIMATE) with the certified partner as majority signatory, or an adviser tool with 20-40 Belgian firms (EUR 100-200k ARR, ESTIMATE) and an NL pilot.
- Year 5: boutique path plateaus at EUR 1-2M (people business, one-off clients, regional); platform path needs NL+FR and USD-style funding to matter — and competes with Keye/Termina-class tools moving down-market and with Companyweb/Altares adding analytics.
Can it escape founder labour? As a service, no — it is a boutique whose economics depend on a licence the founder does not hold. As tooling, possibly, but the moat is thin (public data + LLM) and the buyers (accountants) are conservative and already served by Companyweb (ITAA/IBR official supplier).

## 16. Main risks and reasons NOT to pursue; evidence that would flip the recommendation

Reasons not to pursue: (1) art. 3, 5° reservation + active ITAA prosecution — a solo non-member selling target financial analysis is exposed to criminal complaint, and ITAA's 2025 stance is escalating; (2) Analyzediz and Syno M&A show the "affordable independent small-deal DD" wedge is already occupied in Flanders, and NL mid-tier productised it in 2026; (3) one-off customers: all repeat depends on advisers who are themselves ITAA/IBR members or brokers with conflicts; (4) part-time incompatibility with two-week SLAs; (5) AI commoditises exactly the part the founder would do, leaving the part he cannot sign; (6) FR-only reach excludes the largest and best-priced market (Flanders); (7) Belgian price evidence for small-deal DD is still thin (one NL-language EUR 2-20k band; FR forfaits EUR 10-25k).
Evidence that would change the recommendation: a written legal opinion (or ITAA guidance) that buyer-side "financial analysis / QoE" framed as management consulting is outside art. 3 when no accounting documents are "verified and corrected"; a certified accountant or reviseur willing to co-brand and sign at a 20-30% share; WE confirming the founder can be labelled; three brokers/accountants each committing 4+ referrals a year at EUR 5k; or, for the tooling path, 5 Belgian advisers paying for the NBB/ledger tool within 60 days of a demo.
