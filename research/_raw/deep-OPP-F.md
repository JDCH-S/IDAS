## 1. Exact problem and exact customer

## 10. AI architecture and build effort
- **Evidence library schema:** entity (supplier) → evidence objects {type: policy | certificate | invoice-derived metric | HR metric | insurance | MSP report | contract clause; period; owner; source file; confidence; expiry} → mapped to canonical "fact atoms" (e.g., scope-1 tCO2e FY2025, MFA coverage %, backup RPO, ISO 9001 cert no./expiry) → mapped to framework nodes (VSME Basic B1-B11, CyFun Basic 34 controls, DORA Art. 30 clause list, EcoVadis themes).
- **Questionnaire parsing:** ingest xlsx/docx/pdf/portal screenshots; LLM classifies each question to framework nodes + answer type (yes/no, number, narrative, attachment). Buyer-specific format templates stored per buyer (the cross-client asset).
- **Retrieval + drafting:** hybrid retrieval (BM25 + embeddings) over fact atoms and source chunks; answer generated with mandatory citations to evidence IDs; confidence score; "not in library" flag → task for client/MSP.
- **Human review:** founder/partner reviews all security answers and any answer <0.8 confidence; CEO signs; audit trail of who approved what (needed for liability).
- **Export:** fill the buyer's xlsx/docx; for portals (EcoVadis, osapiens, IntegrityNext) produce copy-paste packs; later browser automation.
- **Build effort (ESTIMATE):** manual phase: Notion/Airtable + Claude/GPT with structured prompts, 0 code, 2 weekends. Semi-automated: Python + LlamaIndex/LangChain or paper-qa, Postgres/pgvector, Streamlit review UI — 150-300 founder-hours over 4-6 months at 10-15 h/week; a contract developer EUR 5-15k. Multi-tenant SaaS with portal exports and SSO: EUR 50-150k or a technical co-founder.
- **OSS/commodity risk:** HIGH. Cited RAG with review UI is what 1up (USD 300/mo), Conveyor, Inventive, Vanta ship; Azure publishes a reference sample; the Orbiq checklist is already offered "as Markdown for AI agents". The defensible parts are the *curated fact atoms*, the *Belgian buyer-format templates* and the *MSP/accountant-validated evidence*, not the pipeline.

## 11. AI leverage / commoditisation / AGI / ASI tests
- **AI leverage test:** does AI change the unit economics? YES, moderately — drafting time per questionnaire falls from ~8-15 h to ~2-4 h review (ESTIMATE from vendor claims; no Belgian data). It turns a consultancy into a productised service, not into software. Classification: **MEANINGFUL, NOT TRANSFORMATIVE.**
- **AI commoditisation test:** is the AI part reproducible by anyone with the same tools? YES — RAG over documents is table stakes; every incumbent has it; frontier models will read a questionnaire and a folder of PDFs natively. Classification: **COMMODITY** — value must come from evidence curation, channel and buyer acceptance.
- **AGI test (5-yr, models reliably do multi-step knowledge work):** the founder's review layer and the accountant's data prep shrink; the buyer side automates validation (68% already use AI); questionnaires may be replaced by machine-readable disclosures (VSME digital template, DPP). The service phase erodes; the *verified evidence registry* survives if buyers trust it. Classification: **EXPOSED unless it becomes a trust/registry business.**
- **ASI test:** irrelevant to timing; regulation, liability and human signature persist longer than the drafting task. Classification: **NEUTRAL.**
- Honest summary: the AI is a productivity tool inside a compliance-services business. It is not an AI moat.

## 11b. Red team — five strongest reasons this fails
1. **The bundle has no buyer persona and low ARPA (SERIOUS).** Evidence: every incumbent picks one pillar; UNIZO survey shows suppliers already pay "tens of thousands" for portals/consultants yet no bundler exists; non-tech CEOs buy relief per crisis, not a subscription. Verdict: SERIOUS — mitigated by per-questionnaire pricing and a crisis-led wedge.
2. **Sunhat / 1up move down-market before the founder has 20 clients (SERIOUS).** Evidence: Sunhat EUR 9.2M Series A, "10x in 15 months", Gartner Cool Vendor; 1up USD 300/mo. Verdict: SERIOUS for the SaaS ambition; MANAGEABLE for a Belgian service niche (they will not do Dutch/French evidence prep for a 80-FTE metal shop).
3. **The value-chain cap and free sector tools shrink the ESG pain (MANAGEABLE).** Evidence: cap = VSME Basic ~46 data points from FY2027; VLAIO Excel, Fenavian/Workable Method, Eevery free tier. Verdict: MANAGEABLE — the cap standardises the input (good for a library) while cutting consultancy hours; ESG pillar becomes low-price.
4. **Security attestation without security credentials (SERIOUS, potentially FATAL if handled wrong).** Evidence: NIS2 questionnaires require executive signature; CCB "CyFun Basic for suppliers"; ECP already equips MSPs; only 35% of BE consultants insured. A founder who drafts "we have MFA everywhere" without an MSP verifying it is a liability event. Verdict: SERIOUS → MANAGEABLE only with a contractual MSP partner who owns security answers.
5. **Founder capacity and channel speed (SERIOUS).** Evidence: 10-15 h/week; 3,676 tiny accounting offices; Belgian sales cycles via federations are slow; each questionnaire needs hours of review. Verdict: SERIOUS — realistic ceiling ~EUR 3-8k/month as a side business before automation or a partner; not venture-scale without leaving employment.
Overall: no single FATAL reason; three SERIOUS structural ones (persona/ARPA, funded competition, founder capacity) that together cap this as a niche service unless buyer acceptance is won.

## 16. Main risks; reasons NOT to pursue; evidence that would change the recommendation
