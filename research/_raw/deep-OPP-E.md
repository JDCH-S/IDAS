## 1. Exact problem and exact customer
**Problem (VERIFIED, multiple sources):** DORA (in force 17 Jan 2025) does not regulate small ICT vendors directly, but forces their financial-entity (FE) customers to push Article 28-30 obligations down by contract; vendors receive (a) DORA questionnaires of 30-80 questions (governance, security, BCP, sub-outsourcing, exit) with ~2-week deadlines, (b) bank-drafted "DORA addenda" often exceeding what DORA strictly requires, and (c) recurring data requests for the FE's Register of Information (LEI, subcontractor chain, data locations, criticality). Sources: https://www.workstreet.com/blog/dora-compliance ; https://www.regulation-dora.eu/blog/dora-third-party-ict-providers-guide-for-suppliers ; https://louwersadvocaten.nl/dora-compliance-voor-ict-dienstverleners-dit-zijn-de-aandachtspunten/ ("many institutions use DORA to renegotiate existing contracts, often going beyond what is strictly required") ; https://dorapilot.com/ ("reply to your bank's DORA assessment in 2 hours, not 60").
- Hardest clause for SaaS standard terms: Art. 30(3)(e) unrestricted audit/access/inspection rights for the FE, its appointed third party and the competent authority — pooled reports/questionnaires do not satisfy it (VERIFIED, Atlant Security). https://atlantsecurity.com/blog/dora-for-saas-companies
- Subcontracting RTS (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/532, OJ 2 Jul 2025, in force 22 Jul 2025) requires FEs to assess and contractually control the subcontracting chain for ICT services supporting critical/important functions — vendors must therefore disclose and manage their own subcontractor chain (VERIFIED). https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2025/07/published-in-oj-commission-delegated-regulation-eu-2025-532-supplementing-dora-with-regard-to-rts-specifying-the-elements-that-a-financial-entity-has-to-determine-and-assess-when-subcontracting-ic/ ; https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/07/uniform-standards-for-ict-subcontracting-in-eu-financial-sector-new-obligations
- Vendor-side pain is real at buyer level too: PwC survey (cited by Silicon Luxembourg) — half of FEs saw issues with their ICT providers' DORA compliance; 1 in 4 expect to terminate contracts with ICT providers over risk-compliance issues; 58% said their ICT providers had significant areas to address (VERIFIED search-summary). https://www.siliconluxembourg.lu/dora-early-challenges-for-fintechs-and-ict-providers/

**Exact customer (INFERENCE from the above):**
- Size: ICT vendors with 5-50 employees (DoraPilot's own targeting: "SaaS, dev shops, infra providers with 5-50 employees who keep getting DORA questionnaires from bank clients and have no compliance team"). https://dorapilot.com/
- Sector: SaaS, managed-service/hosting, software houses, data/KYC/regtech tools, outsourced dev shops selling to banks, insurers, IFMs/fund administrators (LU), payment/e-money institutions, investment firms.
- Who signs: founder/CEO or COO; in 20-50 FTE firms the Head of Sales or a "compliance-by-accident" CTO. No compliance officer exists (that is the segment definition).
- Trigger events: (1) first DORA questionnaire or bank-drafted addendum arriving mid-deal or at renewal; (2) renewal cycle where the FE re-papers contracts; (3) FE's annual RoI refresh request (LEI, subcontractors, data locations); (4) a lost or stalled deal ("security questionnaire is the most common reason enterprise B2B SaaS deals stall in the final mile" — osto.one, generic not DORA-specific). https://www.osto.one/resources/blog/security-questionnaire-killed-enterprise-deal/
- Not the customer: vendors already on Vanta/Drata with SOC 2/ISO 27001 and an in-house compliance lead (they will self-serve), and the 19 CTPPs.

## 6. Why now; why nobody productised it; why buy from this founder
- Why now (VERIFIED): DORA live since 17 Jan 2025; first RoI cycle Apr 2025; second cycle Mar 2026 with 40% LU compliance by mid-March; subcontracting RTS in force 22 Jul 2025 (chain disclosure now contractual); 2026 is "the year supervision turned from guidance into examination" (vendor commentary); 19 CTPPs designated Nov 2025 focuses FEs on the long tail. https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2025/07/published-in-oj-commission-delegated-regulation-eu-2025-532-supplementing-dora-with-regard-to-rts-specifying-the-elements-that-a-financial-entity-has-to-determine-and-assess-when-subcontracting-ic/ ; https://regreportingdesk.com/dora-register-of-information/
- Counter-signal (VERIFIED): FSMA and other NCAs moved to a "limited update / confirm unchanged" 2026 cycle — the regulator-driven annual refresh is softer than the thesis assumes. https://www.fsma.be/en/news/dora-register-information-third-party-ict-service-providers-limited-update-2026
- Why nobody productised it (INFERENCE): (1) someone did — DoraPilot (Irish, launched ~2025/26, intro pricing for first 50 teams suggests very early, ENF traction); (2) law firms earn more per hour on the bank side; (3) GRC platforms price for 50+ FTE; (4) the addendum is freely copyable so the "product" collapses to a data sheet + evidence index that looks too thin to VC-fund; (5) low recurrence.
- Why a vendor would buy from a non-lawyer, non-security founder (INFERENCE, weak until interviewed): the buyer of this pack is a founder/COO who wants the bank off their back before renewal; they buy "done, accepted by the bank" not credentials. Credibility levers available: (a) partner law firm's name on the addendum; (b) FP&A/reporting background maps directly to the RoI's data-model logic (LEI, taxonomy, consolidation levels) — the thing FEs most often got wrong in the dry run; (c) a DORA Foundation/Practitioner certificate (EUR 535-1,665); (d) evidence of packs accepted by named BE/LU banks. Weakness: any security question beyond documentation (pen-test results, TLPT support, Art. 30(3)(e) audit mechanics) exposes the founder — must be scoped out or referred.

## 10. AI architecture and build effort
Scope note: no LLM vendor or model pricing is asserted here (not verified this session); costs are labelled ESTIMATE.
Pipeline (INFERENCE / design):
1. Intake: web form or shared folder; vendor uploads MSA/T&Cs, DPA, ISO 27001/SOC 2 reports or self-assessment, BCP/DR test summary, cyber-insurance certificate, subcontractor/sub-processor list, hosting/data-location statement, org chart, LEI (or none).
2. Extraction agent: LLM with structured-output schema → fills the RoI-relevant fields of the ESAs' ITS templates (provider identity: LEI/EUID, parent, country; ICT service type per the ESAs taxonomy; data locations; subcontracting chain with rank; substitutability; criticality flags as *proposed*, since criticality is the FE's determination). LEI resolution via public GLEIF lookup (no auth needed). Confidence scores + "source page" citation per field for human review.
3. Gap mapper: rule-based checklist of Art. 30(2) and 30(3) items + subcontracting RTS elements; LLM classifies each clause of the vendor's existing contract as present/partial/absent, producing a mapping chart (cf. HYPR's public Art. 30 mapping chart format) and the list of positions the vendor must commit to (audit rights, incident support, exit, subcontracting notice).
4. Assembly: templated DOCX/PDF generation (python-docx) — (a) Art. 30 addendum from the partner firm's template with vendor-specific fills; (b) RoI-ready data sheet (also exportable as CSV in the ITS column names); (c) evidence index; (d) 1-page cover note for the bank; (e) bilingual FR/EN (NL later).
5. Review: founder reviews every field (mandatory in year 1); law-firm review of the addendum where the vendor buys that option.
6. Refresh agent: annual diff — re-ingest changed docs, flag changed subcontractors/locations, regenerate.
Build effort (ESTIMATE): manual pack in Word/Excel: 0 build. Semi-automated (forms + LLM extraction script + DOCX templates): 60-120 founder-hours over 2-3 months at 10-15 h/week, feasible for a Python learner. Self-serve web product with accounts, payments, xBRL validation: 400-800 hours or a contractor (EUR 15-40k) — NOT recommended (DoraPilot/DoraPass already cover it). Running cost per pack (LLM + hosting): low single-digit euros (ESTIMATE).
Do-not-build: RoI xBRL-CSV validators (Arelle is open source; DoraPass sells validation at EUR 500/yr), GRC control monitoring, trust-centre hosting.

## 11. AI leverage / commoditisation / AGI / ASI tests
- AI leverage test (does AI make the founder 3-10x more productive?): PASS-PARTIAL. Extraction and mapping cut a pack from ~10-12 h manual to ~4-6 h (ESTIMATE); the residual is human: chasing the vendor's documents and negotiating the bank's red-lines. Classification: **moderate leverage**.
- AI commoditisation test (can a competitor with an LLM replicate the offer at near-zero marginal cost?): FAIL. A law firm with an LLM can generate the addendum and mapping tomorrow; DoraPilot already sells questionnaire answers + RoI export at EUR 29-49/month; public addenda are free. Classification: **already commoditised at the artefact level**; only the local service wrapper (bank-facing handling, bilingual, three-regulator know-how) resists, and only for ~2-3 years.
- AGI test (if models reliably do expert-level legal/compliance work): the artefacts become worthless; the founder's role collapses to accountability/insurance (the "signature"), which he cannot provide without a bar licence or CISO credential. Classification: **no AGI-resilient moat**.
- ASI test: irrelevant/dominated; regulation itself would change faster than the business.
- Honest verdict: this is an AI-*enabled* service where the AI lowers the founder's cost, not the customer's switching cost. Defensibility must come from bank acceptance and references, not the pipeline.
