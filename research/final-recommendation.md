# Final recommendation

Date: 2026-09-12. This is the decision document. Evidence trail: `opportunity-universe.md` (102 opportunities, 16-theme market map), `competitive-analysis.md`, `top-20.md`, `top-5-deep-dives.md`, `rejected-opportunities.md`, `opportunity-database.csv/.json`, `_raw/` (five discovery streams, four existence-check batches, six deep dives, four red teams, one independent final review), `sources.md` (1,400+ URLs and the method's limits: every fact came from search-result summaries because pages could not be opened; confirm load-bearing facts at the URL before acting).

## 1. The honest headline

**None of the 102 opportunities is "exceptional" on the evidence, and the research is confident about why.** Of the 40 checked for existence, 12 were already built by incumbents and 16 are software layers that are crowded or being absorbed by platform owners (Odoo, Microsoft, Visma/Silverfin, Exact, Xero) and by the Ghent accounting-tech cluster (Silverfin, Ravical, Dytto, Cashfeed, Eagl, TenderWolf) plus the Brussels data layer (Chift, Peliqan). The six that reached deep research each lost their product leg under red-team scrutiny: Odoo 19/20 and EUR 39-per-entity SaaS for consolidation; the Belgian Peppol Authority's own scanner (identifier errors 2.41% to 0.31% in four months) for Peppol operations; Kube ESG, the free national VSME passport backed by Isabel, Febelfin and the four big banks, for the supplier passport; DoraPilot at EUR 29-49/month for the DORA pack; Analyzediz, Syno and the ITAA reserved-activity law for QoE-light; suspended primes, a free public facilitator and a slipped calendar for co-ownership packs.

**The one moat the programme found in 102 ideas is the licence.** Every solo service a non-ITAA founder could sell hit article 3 of the law of 17 March 2019 (bookkeeping, closing, annual accounts, verification of accounting documents, and "analysis by accounting techniques of the situation and functioning of undertakings" are reserved; illegal practice is criminal and prosecuted). The independent final review then established two facts the team had found but under-used: a registered accounting firm only needs ITAA professionals to hold the **majority** of voting rights and board seats, so a non-member can co-found and hold a minority; and the **seven-year-experience route** waives the admission exam and the internship (the aptitude exam remains; an exemption simulator went live on 10 July 2026). No Belgian AI-native accounting or controlling *firm* surfaced in any search, while Neno (Amsterdam, EUR 6.6M seed in August 2026) names Belgium in its 2027 wave. That is the only venture-shaped, reachable, defensible slot the research can point to, and it is a hypothesis, not a validated opportunity.

So the recommendation has two layers, as the final review proposed: **a cash engine that can start in 30 days for under EUR 500, and one venture experiment run in parallel for EUR 0-100 that tests whether the founder can become, or co-own, the licence.**

## 3. THE ONE: OPP-A as the cash engine, plus the ITAA-partnered agent-first firm as the venture experiment

**Why OPP-A.** It is the only opportunity where the founder's own skills are the product (group reporting, HQ formats, intercompany discipline, plus the ability to build agent tooling), where the buyer is not an accounting firm already courted by the Ghent startups, where incumbents are people-priced (freelance controllers EUR 600-800/day, interim CFOs EUR 900-1,400/day, Big-4 managed services) and structurally unwilling to compress, where the first customer can come from the founder's network within 90 days, and where every hour of tooling is reusable. It was chosen because it survived two red teams, not because it excites: the red teams killed only its product ambition and its monthly-cadence assumption.

**Why the venture experiment.** Because "an agent-first firm with an ITAA signatory" is the single path to a large company the research could identify, and the programme had rejected it at the screen (as U-019) without investigating it. The final review's evidence (majority rule for professionals, seven-year exemption, no Belgian AI-native firm, Neno's 2027 clock, 56% of firms refusing clients) makes it a testable hypothesis that costs nothing OPP-A does not already need.

- **Background fit:** exact for OPP-A; for the firm, the founder is the minority tech/ops and controlling partner, not the signatory, unless the exemption route makes him one within 12-18 months.
- **Interests:** AI agents on finance data; B2B conversations with finance peers; building a firm that runs on tooling rather than headcount.
- **Belgium:** dense holding and subsidiary population; Odoo is Belgian; controller and accountant shortage in all three regions; Brussels HQ concentration; French works in Brussels, Wallonia and Luxembourg.
- **Europe:** Luxembourg second (consolidation-as-a-service already exists there on fixed fees; fiduciaire rates EUR 50-95/h; the founder has no network there yet); France third; the Netherlands only with a Dutch partner.
- **Time:** 10-15 h/week fits a quarterly tier and 4-6 clients; monthly work collides with the founder's own close week unless the client's deadline is WD10 or later.
- **Capital:** EUR 500-2,000 for OPP-A; EUR 0-100 for the experiment; EUR 5-20k to capitalise a firm if the experiment succeeds.
- **AI trajectory:** AI is leverage (3-5x founder productivity), not moat. The window is the 3-7 years in which agents are good enough to use but customers still need a named human who owns the numbers.
- **AGI/ASI:** the labour revenue is destroyed; what remains is an accountable, insured, licensed signatory position and client relationships. The firm route is the only way to hold that position.
- **Competition and barriers:** entry is easy for anyone; the licence is the only barrier that becomes a moat.
- **Distribution:** own network first; PE-backed portfolio companies, Odoo integrators and capacity-constrained ITAA firms second.
- **Upside:** OPP-A solo EUR 70-200k/yr at best; the firm EUR 1-3M/yr in five years if it works (HYPOTHESIS), with the Neno-style capital route open if the model proves out.

**Strongest argument FOR.** The founder can bill for a version of his own job within 90 days at 85%+ gross margin with EUR 500 at risk, learn exactly which finance workflows agents absorb, and use the same three gates (legal read, employer authorisation, exemption question) to test whether he can co-own or hold the one licence that software cannot commoditise, in a slot that is empty in Belgium and on a 12-18-month clock.

**Strongest argument AGAINST.** OPP-A alone is a second income, not a startup: no Belgian SME group was found paying monthly for outsourced group reporting; the platform owner ships the consolidation feature; freelance controllers compete at EUR 600-800/day; the founder's close week collides with the clients'. And the firm is a hypothesis built on legal facts, untested with a single accountant: partner economics, governance, the aptitude exam, Neno's arrival and the ITAA's own repositioning ("AI prepares, the accountant decides") could all go against it.

**Biggest unknown.** Whether a certified accountant with a client stop will co-found an agent-first firm with a non-member minority partner, and, for OPP-A, whether small exempt Belgian groups will pay for outsourced group reporting at any cadence.

**Cheapest way to test the unknown.** (1) Submit the ITAA seven-year-exemption eligibility question with CV and task description (free); (2) ten conversations with certified accountants aged roughly 30-45 who run 2-10-person practices in Brussels, Walloon Brabant or Namur and are refusing clients, pitching a professionally-majority-owned, agent-first firm; (3) a written legal read on art. 3 scope for management reporting and intercompany matching (EUR 500-1,500), plus a free written question to the ITAA service desk and an insurance-broker quote with the scope description; (4) ten trigger interviews with owners or finance leads of 3-8-entity groups and PE-backed portfolio companies on cadence, current producer of group numbers and spend.

**What would make me abandon it.** OPP-A: the legal read classifies the scope as reserved with no firm willing to subcontract; the employer refuses written authorisation; a majority of the ten interviews say quarterly numbers from the accountant are enough and no PE-backed or HQ-mandated client appears; or two client deadlines are missed because of the day job. The firm: exemption ineligible AND none of ten accountants willing to discuss a term sheet; or Odoo 20 and the ITAA's own tooling remove the reporting layer the firm would sell.

## 4. Final startup plan

### 4.1 OPP-A, the cash engine

**Product.** A fixed-fee "group reporting and intercompany discipline" service: ledger data assembly from Odoo/Exact/Yuki or trial-balance exports, maintained mapping to a group chart of accounts, intercompany matching lists and proposed eliminations (the client's bookkeeper posts them), consolidated management P&L, balance sheet and cash flow, a pack in the client's or HQ's format with management narrative on figures the client owns, tie-out QA, a 30-60 minute review call. Quarterly entry tier; monthly only with a WD10+ deadline or a buyer-mandated calendar. Explicit contractual exclusions: bookkeeping, posting, statutory consolidated accounts, creditworthiness or risk analysis in the art. 3, 5 sense.

**Customer.** (a) Owner-CEO or family CFO of a Brussels, Walloon or Luxembourg holding with 3-8 entities on Odoo Enterprise or Exact, with a trigger (bank covenant, acquisition, new investor, auditor remark, controller departure). (b) PE-backed buy-and-build portfolio companies, where the fund mandates monthly reporting. (c) Finance manager or managing director of a foreign-owned Belgian or Luxembourg subsidiary whose HQ has no EPM, D365 or SAP, outside the founder's employer's sector (a generic caution; the employer's identity is not known to this research).

**Problem.** Group numbers nobody inside owns: Excel consolidation, intercompany that does not reconcile, HQ packs produced in overtime; the alternatives cost EUR 600-800 per controller day or ~EUR 90-100k for a hire they cannot find.

**Existing alternatives.** Owner's Excel; accountant once a year; interim controller; Big-4 managed services; NL SaaS run by the client's own staff.

**Competitive wedge.** Done-for-you, fixed-fee, below two interim days, delivered by an ex-corporate controller with agent tooling and the accountability that SaaS does not carry.

**MVP.** One client served by hand: exports, Excel mapping, a Python intercompany matching script, the pack in the client's template, LLM-drafted commentary reviewed line by line, a review call.

**Manual version.** Everything above for the first two clients and the first two periods of every client.

**AI layer.** Deterministic: FX, aggregation, intercompany matching rules, eliminations, bridges, Excel/PowerPoint rendering, QA rules, audit log. LLM (enterprise or zero-retention terms, account-level aggregates only): mapping proposals with confidence, intercompany exception classification and email drafts, commentary from computed bridges, pack tie-out reading. Human-in-the-loop on every mapping, elimination and signature.

**Technical architecture.** Read-only connectors (OSS or App Store Odoo MCP; Exact REST with OAuth and a 5,000 requests/day cap; Yuki API; CSV drop folder; optional Peliqan or Chift as a supplier), a Postgres/DuckDB schema per client, Python jobs, openpyxl/python-pptx renderers. Build effort ~170-240 h over 4-6 months at 10 h/week; do not start the ingestion stack before Odoo 20 has been seen (24-26 Sep 2026); no engineer needed until a multi-tenant product, which is not planned.

**Customer acquisition.** Customer #1: an ex-colleague who is finance manager of a non-competing subsidiary, or a family group known through the founder's ecosystem, on a 3-month pilot at EUR 1,000-1,500/month with free onboarding for a reference. Customers #2-10: two PE funds running Belgian buy-and-build platforms, two or three Odoo integrators in Brussels/Wallonia, two or three ITAA firms with client stops (subcontracting under their engagement letter, the Court of Cassation 28 Nov 2022 route), LinkedIn content in French and English on Odoo consolidation and intercompany checklists, one Luxembourg fiduciary. Scaling: Odoo partner events, BrightAnalytics/EMAsphere implementation partnership, bank relationship managers, Big-4 alumni and HQ-controller networks.

**Pricing.** Quarterly tier EUR 900-1,500 per quarter; monthly EUR 1,500-2,500 (holdings) and EUR 2,000-3,500 (subsidiaries and portfolio companies); onboarding EUR 2,500-5,000. Why: below two interim days (EUR 1,200-1,600), far below a hire, above the SaaS floor (EUR 400-900/month plus the client's labour); the outcome-based fee keeps the tooling gains with the founder.

**Initial costs.** Registration as complementary self-employed ~EUR 109 plus VAT activation ~EUR 60-80 (deferred until the first paid pilot); professional liability insurance ~EUR 270-1,000/yr; legal read EUR 500-1,500; LLM credits EUR 50-200/month; optional connector or SaaS subscription EUR 40-200/month. Total to first revenue EUR 500-2,000. Social contributions: minimum EUR 98.51/quarter, exemption below EUR 1,922 annual income. Start under the VAT franchise (turnover under EUR 25,000); incorporate a BV/SRL (EUR 1,500-2,500) around EUR 80k turnover, or earlier if the firm experiment succeeds. Client-side subsidies should not be counted on: Flanders' consulting subsidy is cyber-only since Feb 2026, Brussels' is suspended, and Wallonia's vouchers require a labelled provider with three years' track record.

**Legal and regulatory checks.** Art. 3 and art. 117 of the law of 17 March 2019; ITAA guidance on subcontracting after the Court of Cassation ruling of 28 Nov 2022 and the 2022 discipline cases against members cooperating with non-members (so any ITAA partnership must be pure subcontracting under the firm's engagement letter, or co-ownership under the majority rule); the ITAA seven-year-experience exemption; the employment contract and work rules (loyalty duty, side-activity clauses; obtain written authorisation with sector exclusions); GDPR art. 28 processor agreement, EU hosting, LLM data terms; NDAs and HQ security questionnaires; professional liability wording covering the intended scope; VAT franchise and the Peppol e-invoicing status of a franchise business (commonly reported as out of scope for issuing; confirm).

### 4.2 The venture experiment: an ITAA-partnered, agent-first controlling and accounting firm

**Thesis (HYPOTHESIS built on VERIFIED facts).** A jointly owned firm in which certified accountants hold the majority of votes and board seats as the 2019 law requires, that takes the client stops of Belgian SME groups with agent tooling and the founder's group-reporting practice; the founder is the minority tech/ops partner and stays employed until revenue justifies otherwise, and pursues the seven-year exemption to become a signatory himself. Neno's EUR 80/month AI-native bookkeeping shows the investable version one layer down; the Belgian slot is empty and the clock is 12-18 months.

**30-day test (EUR 0-100, 15-20 hours).**
1. Week 1: submit the ITAA seven-year-exemption eligibility question with CV and task description (the art. 14 form; the simulator is live). Outcome A: the founder can sit the aptitude exam within 12-18 months. Outcome B: he needs a partner.
2. Weeks 1-4: ten conversations with certified accountants (ITAA, roughly 30-45, own practice of 2-10 staff, Brussels/Walloon Brabant/Namur, refusing clients) sourced via LinkedIn, one AccountancyVandaag or ITAA regional event, and the founder's own accountant. Pitch the professionally-majority-owned, agent-first firm.
3. Week 4: one written term-sheet sketch (ownership, governance, EUR 5-20k capital, the founder's OPP-A clients as the first controlling book).
Success: exemption eligible OR at least two accountants willing to discuss a term sheet, AND Odoo 20 does not remove the reporting layer the firm would sell. Kill: exemption ineligible AND none of ten interested; then OPP-A stays a side income and the 2028 e-reporting reconciliation engine becomes the 2027 build.

### 4.3 Combined 30-day plan
1. Days 1-3: read the employment contract and work rules; draft the written authorisation request for "financial reporting services to non-competing SMEs outside [sector]".
2. Days 1-7: ITAA service-desk question on art. 3 scope; brief a lawyer for a written read (EUR 500-1,500); insurance-broker quote with the scope description; submit the seven-year-exemption eligibility question.
3. Days 3-21: ten trigger interviews (owners or finance leads of 3-8-entity groups and PE-backed portfolio companies, via two Odoo partners, one PE fund and one ITAA firm; French or English, Dutch only if the founder has it) and five subsidiary finance managers; ten accountant conversations for the firm experiment.
4. Days 10-25: Odoo Experience (24-26 Sep 2026, Brussels): Odoo 20 finance sessions; ask two Belgian Odoo partners how many multi-company clients lack a controller and what they charge for reporting.
5. Days 20-30: offer one OPP-A pilot; deliver the first pack manually; log every hour; write the term-sheet sketch if the accountant conversations warrant it.
Success at day 30: authorisation in hand or clearly obtainable; legal read not prohibitive; one committed pilot; at least three interviewees confirming a quarterly or monthly budget line; exemption eligibility known; at least two accountants willing to continue.

### 4.4 Combined 90-day plan
1. Months 1-2: pilot #1 delivered for one period; engagement letter with exclusions, DPA and NDA templates in place; intercompany matching script and mapping table built around the pilot only after Odoo 20 has been seen.
2. Month 2: two-quote price test with three prospects (EUR 2.5k/month outcome pack versus three controller days at EUR 700); two LinkedIn pieces (FR/EN); referral conversation with one Odoo integrator, one PE fund and one ITAA firm; decision on the firm term sheet.
3. Month 3: pilot #2 in another segment; trial-balance ingestion and pack rendering automated for the two clients; hours per pack measured; decision on the ITAA exemption filing, on the firm, and on whether to keep any product leg.
Success at day 90: two paying OPP-A clients at EUR 1,000/month or more, no missed deadline, hours per pack falling; either a signed intent with an accountant co-founder or an exemption route with a date, or a documented kill of the firm hypothesis.

### 4.5 Validation thresholds
- KEEP GOING: legal read allows the scope (own name or via subcontracting); employer authorises; at least two paying clients by day 90; at least three of ten interviews confirm a budget line; hours per pack under 8 by month 3; a channel partner refers; for the firm, exemption eligible or two accountants at term-sheet stage.
- PIVOT: legal read allows only subcontracting and a firm offers acceptable terms (become that firm's group-reporting unit and pursue the ITAA route); interviews show quarterly-only demand (sell the quarterly tier, use the freed hours for OPP-C with a signatory or OPP-E); Odoo 20 closes any residual product gap (drop the product leg for good); the firm experiment fails but the exemption route is open (pursue the exam, keep OPP-A).
- ABANDON: legal read classifies the scope as reserved with no firm willing to subcontract or co-own; employer refuses; fewer than two paying clients after six months of active selling; two missed client deadlines caused by the day job; firm experiment killed and exemption ineligible.

## 5. Why not the others, in one line each
- OPP-B Peppol operations: the authority's own scanner and vendor status views removed the problem and the moat; keep the EUR 1-2k incident-documentation engagement as a door-opener with fiduciaires and the 2028 reconciliation engine as a 2027 build.
- OPP-C QoE-light: excellent skill fit and real demand, but the core deliverable is a reserved activity under active prosecution; it belongs inside the firm experiment.
- OPP-D co-ownership packs: policy-dependent, free-channel-occupied, no scale.
- OPP-E DORA pack: real but one-off, small and already productised by DoraPilot; a side service once a law-firm contact exists.
- OPP-F supplier passport: Kube ESG, EcoVadis Vitals and CyFun Small occupy the passport layer; the founder has neither security credibility nor Dutch.
- Everything else: see `rejected-opportunities.md` (52 rejected at screening, 12 at existence check, 6 demoted at deep research and red team).

## 6. Corrections made after the independent review
- The "Dosign equal-pay ruling" used as a "why now" pillar in earlier drafts is the Dutch Hoge Raad WAADI judgment (27 Sep 2024), imported via a recruitment-agency blog; it does not apply to Belgian freelance controllers and has been removed from the argument. The "interim rates rising" tailwind is unproven.
- The Odoo consolidation gap is "unsettled until 26 Sep 2026", not "live" or "closed": Odoo 19 documentation describes a consolidation module with auto-elimination of consistently coded intercompany accounts, while 2026 forum threads still ask how to do eliminations; Odoo 20 is unveiled at Odoo Experience.
- The red team's premise that the founder's employer is a foreign-owned subsidiary is not in the brief; the sector carve-out stays as generic caution.
- Client-side subsidy claims for OPP-A onboarding were removed: Wallonia's vouchers require a labelled provider with roughly three years' track record.
- The legal-read budget is stated consistently as EUR 500-1,500; the OPP-C legal opinion on art. 3, 5 may cost more (EUR 2-4k) because it is a formal opinion, not a read.
- The ranking in `top-5-deep-dives.md` now matches this file: OPP-A, OPP-C, OPP-E, then OPP-B and OPP-D as reserves, OPP-F as a further reserve.
- Interview plans are French-first; Flemish interviews only if the founder has Dutch.
- U-019 ("BE/LU agent-native bookkeeping firm"), rejected at the screen, is reopened as the venture experiment in its recombined form.

## 7. What the research could not settle, and how to close it
- Belgian counts of groups on Odoo/Exact without a controller and of foreign-owned subsidiaries without an EPM: no public source (Statbel's ~5,500 groups is on a 2015 basis and sits uneasily beside 30,552 holdings and 80,210 management companies); NBB consolidated-filing counts would arbitrate; interviews and partner intel close the rest.
- Whether "management reporting for third parties" is reserved: only a lawyer's read or an ITAA written answer settles it.
- Whether outsourced group reporting is a budgeted purchase for exempt groups at any cadence: only sales conversations settle it.
- Whether in-house controlling experience counts toward the ITAA seven-year route: the eligibility question settles it.
- Odoo 20's consolidation scope: Odoo Experience, 24-26 Sep 2026.
- The founder's own constraints (employer, sector, Dutch): known to the founder, not to this research; they change the segment mix, not the recommendation.
