# Final recommendation

Date: 2026-09-12. This is the decision document. Evidence trail: `opportunity-universe.md` (102 opportunities), `competitive-analysis.md`, `top-20.md`, `top-5-deep-dives.md`, `rejected-opportunities.md`, `_raw/` (discovery streams, existence checks, deep dives, red teams, final review), `sources.md` (method limits: every fact comes from search-result summaries; pages could not be opened; confirm load-bearing facts at the URL before acting).

## 1. The honest headline

**No opportunity in the 102 investigated qualifies as "exceptional" on the evidence.** The research was designed to kill ideas, and it did: of 40 opportunities checked for existence, 12 were already built by incumbents, 16 are software layers that are crowded or being absorbed by platforms (Microsoft, Odoo, Xero, Exact, Visma), and the 12 that reached deep research all lost their software or venture leg under red-team scrutiny. What survives is a small set of **service-first businesses that a controller can start in 30 days with under EUR 500, that AI makes unusually cheap to run, and that carry option value on a product only if a platform owner leaves a gap open.**

That is a useful result, not a failure. It says: in Belgian SME finance in late 2026, the software rents have been taken by platforms and by the Ghent cluster; the money a part-time, non-ITAA founder can reach is in accountable finance work, sold on triggers, at prices anchored to interim day rates. The path to a large company, if it exists, runs through becoming an agent-first firm with an ITAA signatory or through a product that the research could not validate yet.

## 3. THE ONE: OPP-A, in its steelman form

**Why this one.** It is the only opportunity where the founder holds an asymmetric advantage a generic founder cannot copy (years of corporate group reporting, HQ formats, intercompany discipline, plus the ability to build the agent tooling), where the buyer is not an accounting firm already courted by the Ghent startups, where the incumbents are people-priced and structurally unwilling to compress, where the first customer can come from the founder's own network within 90 days, and where every euro of tooling built is reusable across clients. It is chosen because it survived, not because it excites: its red team confirmed the labour-service core and killed only the product ambition.

- **Background fit:** exact match (controlling, reporting, consolidation, budgeting).
- **Interests:** AI agents applied to finance data; B2B conversations with finance peers; product-from-service strategy.
- **Belgium:** dense holding and subsidiary population; Odoo is Belgian; controller shortage; Brussels HQ concentration; French works for Brussels, Wallonia and Luxembourg.
- **Europe:** Luxembourg is the natural second market; France third (Odoo-heavy, outsourced consolidation is an established category).
- **Time:** 10-15 h/week fits 4-6 clients only with WD10+ or quarterly deadlines and tooling; close-week collision is the biggest operational risk.
- **Capital:** EUR 500-2,000 to first revenue.
- **AI trajectory:** AI is leverage (3-5x founder productivity), not moat; the window is the 3-7 years in which agents are good enough to use but customers still need a named human who owns the numbers.
- **AGI/ASI:** the labour revenue is destroyed; what remains is an accountable, insured, possibly ITAA-qualified signatory position and client relationships. Pursue the ITAA seven-year-experience exemption to turn the regulatory barrier from a threat into a moat.
- **Competition and barriers:** entry is easy for anyone, which is why the plan is to accumulate references, a mapping/IC library and, later, a professional title.
- **Distribution:** own network first; Odoo integrators and capacity-constrained ITAA firms second.
- **Defensibility:** low today; becomes real only with the ITAA route or a product Odoo does not ship.
- **Upside:** EUR 70-200k/yr solo; EUR 1-3M/yr only as an agent-first firm with a signatory, which requires leaving employment and taking capital.

**Strongest argument FOR.** The founder can bill for a version of his own job within 90 days at 85%+ gross margin, with EUR 500 at risk, learning exactly which finance workflows agents can absorb, while the ITAA exemption route can convert experience into a licensed position that no software vendor can commoditise.

**Strongest argument AGAINST.** It is a second income, not a startup: the platform owner (Odoo) and Microsoft ship the consolidation and variance features, four SaaS vendors price the tool layer at EUR 19-119 per entity, exit comparables for the category leader are around USD 70M, and the founder's own close week collides with the clients' close week. If the goal is a venture-scale company, this is a funding engine at best.

**Biggest unknown.** Whether small exempt Belgian groups and no-EPM subsidiaries will pay monthly (not annually) for outsourced group reporting, and whether art. 3, 5 of the 2019 law lets a non-member deliver it in his own name.

**Cheapest way to test the unknown.** (1) A written legal opinion on art. 3 scope (EUR 500-1,500, two weeks) plus a free written question to the ITAA service desk and an insurance-broker quote with the intended scope description; (2) ten trigger interviews with owners or finance leads of 3-8-entity groups sourced through two Odoo partners and one ITAA firm, recording cadence, current producer of group numbers, current spend and the language of the conversation; (3) a two-quote test with three prospects (EUR 2.5k/month outcome pack versus three controller days at EUR 700).

**What would make me abandon it.** The legal opinion classifies management reporting and IC matching for third parties as reserved with no ITAA firm willing to subcontract; the employer refuses written authorisation; a majority of the ten interviews say quarterly numbers from the accountant are enough; or the founder misses a client deadline twice because of the day job.

## 4. Final startup plan (OPP-A)

**Product.** A fixed-fee monthly (or quarterly entry tier) "group reporting and intercompany discipline" service: ledger data assembly from Odoo/Exact/Yuki or trial-balance exports, maintained mapping to a group chart of accounts, intercompany matching lists and proposed eliminations (the client's bookkeeper posts them), consolidated management P&L, balance sheet and cash flow, an HQ-format pack with management narrative on figures the client owns, tie-out QA, a 30-60 minute review call. Explicit exclusions in the contract: bookkeeping, posting, statutory consolidated accounts, creditworthiness or risk analysis in the art. 3, 5 sense.

**Customer.** (a) Owner-CEO or family CFO of a Brussels, Walloon or Luxembourg holding with 3-8 entities on Odoo Enterprise or Exact, with a trigger (bank covenant, acquisition, new investor, auditor remark, controller departure). (b) Local finance manager or managing director of a foreign-owned Belgian or Luxembourg subsidiary whose HQ has no EPM, D365 or SAP, and which is outside the founder's employer's sector.

**Problem.** Group numbers nobody inside owns: Excel consolidation, intercompany that does not reconcile, HQ packs produced in overtime by a 1-3 person team; the alternative costs EUR 600-800 per controller day or ~EUR 90-100k for a hire they cannot find.

**Existing alternatives.** Owner's Excel; accountant once a year; interim controller; Big-4 managed services; NL SaaS run by the client's own staff.

**Competitive wedge.** Done-for-you, monthly or quarterly, fixed-fee, below two interim days, delivered by an ex-corporate controller with agent tooling, with the Odoo/Exact data plumbing that Microsoft's agents do not cover and the accountability that SaaS does not carry.

**MVP.** One client served by hand: exports, Excel mapping, a Python intercompany matching script, the pack in the client's template, LLM-drafted commentary reviewed line by line, a review call.

**Manual version.** Everything above for the first two clients and the first two months of every client.

**AI layer.** Deterministic: FX, aggregation, IC matching rules, eliminations, bridges, Excel/PowerPoint rendering, QA rules, audit log. LLM (enterprise or zero-retention terms, account-level aggregates only): mapping proposals with confidence, IC exception classification and email drafts, commentary from computed bridges, pack tie-out reading. Human-in-the-loop on every mapping, elimination and signature.

**Technical architecture.** Read-only connectors (OSS or App Store Odoo MCP; Exact REST with OAuth, 5,000 requests/day cap; Yuki API; CSV drop folder; optional Peliqan/Chift), Postgres/DuckDB schema per client (entity, period, accounts, versioned mapping, TB lines, IC pairs, eliminations, FX, templates, commentary, QA), Python jobs, openpyxl/python-pptx renderers. Build effort ~170-240 h over 4-6 months at 10 h/week; no engineer needed until a multi-tenant product.

**Customer acquisition.** #1: an ex-colleague who is finance manager of a non-competing Brussels subsidiary, or a family group known through the founder's ecosystem, on a 3-month pilot at EUR 1,000-1,500/month with free onboarding for a reference. #2-10: two or three Odoo integrators in Brussels/Wallonia (white-label onboarding), two or three ITAA firms with client stops (subcontracting under their engagement letter or referral), LinkedIn content in French and English on Odoo consolidation and IC checklists, one Luxembourg fiduciary. Scale: Odoo partner events, EMAsphere/BrightAnalytics implementation partnership, bank relationship managers, Big-4 alumni and HQ-controller networks.

**Pricing.** EUR 1,500-2,500/month (a) and EUR 2,000-3,500/month (b) for monthly cadence; a quarterly entry tier at EUR 900-1,500 per quarter; onboarding EUR 2,500-5,000. Why: below two interim days (EUR 1,200-1,600), far below a hire, above the SaaS floor (EUR 400-900/month plus the client's labour), and the outcome-based pack lets the founder keep the tooling gains.

**Initial costs.** Registration as complementary self-employed ~EUR 109 plus VAT activation ~EUR 60-80 (deferred until the first paid pilot); professional liability insurance ~EUR 270-1,000/yr; legal opinion EUR 500-1,500; LLM credits EUR 50-200/month; optional connector or SaaS subscription EUR 40-200/month. Total to first revenue: EUR 500-2,000. Social contributions: minimum EUR 98.51/quarter, exemption below EUR 1,922 annual income. Start under the VAT franchise (<EUR 25,000); incorporate a BV/SRL (EUR 1,500-2,500) around EUR 80k turnover.

**Legal and regulatory checks (Belgium/EU).** Art. 3 and art. 117 of the law of 17 March 2019 (reserved accounting activities, criminal sanction); ITAA guidance on subcontracting after the Court of Cassation ruling of 28 Nov 2022 and the 2022 discipline cases against members cooperating with non-members; the ITAA seven-year-experience exemption to sit the certified-accountant exam; employment contract and work rules (loyalty duty, side-activity clauses; written authorisation with sector exclusions); GDPR art. 28 processor agreement, EU hosting, LLM data terms; NDA and HQ security questionnaires; professional liability cover with the intended scope description; VAT franchise and Peppol e-invoicing status of a franchise business (commonly reported out of scope for issuing; confirm).

**30-day plan.**
1. Days 1-3: read the employment contract and work rules; draft the written authorisation request for "financial reporting services to non-competing SMEs outside [sector]".
2. Days 1-7: send the art. 3 question to the ITAA service desk; brief a lawyer for a written opinion; request an insurance quote with the scope description; submit the ITAA seven-year-exemption eligibility question with a CV.
3. Days 3-21: ten trigger interviews (owners/finance leads of 3-8-entity groups via two Odoo partners and one ITAA firm) and five subsidiary finance managers; record cadence, producer, spend, language, trigger.
4. Days 10-25: attend Odoo Experience (24-26 Sep 2026, Brussels): Odoo 20 finance sessions and two Belgian Odoo partners on multi-company clients without a controller.
5. Days 20-30: offer one pilot; deliver the first pack manually; log every hour.
Success at day 30: authorisation in hand or clearly obtainable; legal read not prohibitive; one signed or verbally committed pilot; at least three interviewees confirming a monthly or quarterly budget line.

**90-day plan.**
1. Month 1-2: pilot #1 delivered twice; engagement letter with exclusions, DPA and NDA templates in place; IC matching script and mapping table built around the pilot.
2. Month 2: two-quote price test with three prospects; two LinkedIn pieces (FR/EN); referral conversation with one Odoo integrator and one ITAA firm (subcontracting terms, liability, rates).
3. Month 3: pilot #2 in the other segment; trial-balance ingestion and pack rendering automated for the two clients; hours per pack measured; decision on the ITAA exemption filing and on whether to pursue the product leg given Odoo 20.
Success at day 90: two paying clients at >=EUR 1,000/month, no missed deadline, hours per pack falling month on month, one channel partner willing to refer.

**Validation thresholds.**
- KEEP GOING: legal opinion allows the scope (own name or via subcontracting); employer authorises; >=2 paying clients by day 90; >=3 of 10 interviews confirm monthly or quarterly budget; hours per pack below 8 by month 3; a channel partner refers.
- PIVOT: legal opinion allows only subcontracting and a firm offers acceptable terms (become the firm's group-reporting unit and pursue the ITAA route); or interviews show quarterly-only demand (sell the quarterly tier and use the freed hours to test OPP-C with a signatory or OPP-E); or Odoo 20 closes the product gap (drop the product leg, keep the service).
- ABANDON: legal opinion classifies the scope as reserved with no firm willing to subcontract; employer refuses; fewer than two paying clients after six months of active selling; two missed client deadlines caused by the day job.

## 5. Why not the others, in one line each
- OPP-B Peppol operations: the authority's own scanner and vendor status views removed the problem and the moat; keep the EUR 1-2k incident-documentation engagement as a door-opener with fiduciaires.
- OPP-C QoE-light: excellent skill fit, real demand, but selling the core deliverable in the founder's own name is a reserved activity under active prosecution; only with a signatory partner, which makes it the natural second product once an ITAA relationship exists.
- OPP-D co-ownership packs: policy-dependent, free-channel-occupied, no scale.
- OPP-E DORA pack: real but one-off; a good adjacent productised service once a law-firm partner exists.
- OPP-F supplier passport: the one compliance idea with venture upside, but it needs security credibility and a two-sided market the founder is not positioned to build alone.
- Everything else: see `rejected-opportunities.md`.

## 6. What the research could not settle (and how to close it)
- Belgian counts of groups on Odoo/Exact without a controller, and of foreign-owned subsidiaries without an EPM: no public source; close by interviews and partner intel.
- Whether "management reporting for third parties" is reserved: only a lawyer's opinion or an ITAA written answer settles it.
- Whether monthly outsourced group reporting is a budgeted purchase for exempt groups: only sales conversations settle it.
- Odoo 20's exact consolidation scope: settled at Odoo Experience on 24-26 Sep 2026.
- The founder's own constraints (employer, sector, Dutch): known to the founder, not to this research; they change the segment mix, not the recommendation.
